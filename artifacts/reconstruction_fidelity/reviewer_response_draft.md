### Response: quantitative evaluation of replay quality

We thank the reviewer for pointing out that the previous manuscript explained the decoder and replay procedure but did not directly quantify the quality of the reconstructed replay samples. We have added a paired reconstruction-fidelity evaluation at both the replay-event level and the final-buffer level.

Specifically, for each of the 83 replay events during 80,000 meta-training iterations, we randomly sampled 200 additional buffer items solely for evaluation; these samples did not participate in gradient updates. We also evaluated all 1,000 paired real/reconstructed samples in the final replay buffer. All image-space metrics were calculated in a common [0,1] range. We report Pixel MSE, SSIM, cosine similarity obtained from a separately trained and frozen feature extractor, accuracy from an independently trained and frozen classifier on both real and reconstructed samples, and the label preservation rate conditioned on real samples that were correctly classified by the independent classifier.

On the final buffer, Scratch obtained an MSE of 0.0206, SSIM of 0.8221, and frozen-feature cosine similarity of 0.7855; Warm-start obtained 0.0228, 0.8023, and 0.7676, respectively. The reference classifier correctly classified 93.4% of the paired real buffer samples. Its accuracy on reconstructed samples was 0.9% for Scratch and 0.7% for Warm-start, with label preservation rates of 0.96% and 0.64%, respectively.

The event-level analysis shows that structural fidelity improves during training. Comparing the first and last ten events, Scratch improved from 0.0287 to 0.0204 in MSE, from 0.7385 to 0.8241 in SSIM, and from 0.7126 to 0.7867 in frozen-feature cosine similarity. Warm-start showed the same trend. However, the independent semantic measures remained low. Additional analysis showed that the 617 true classes represented in the final buffer were compressed into only 112 predicted classes for Scratch and 87 for Warm-start, indicating that the reconstructions retain coarse structure and feature direction but lose fine-grained class-discriminative strokes.

We have therefore revised the manuscript to narrow our claim. The reconstructed samples preserve structural and coarse representational information, but they should not be interpreted as photorealistic or fully class-faithful reproductions. We explicitly report this limitation and include the complete event-level curves and final-buffer analysis. We believe this additional quantitative evaluation provides a more direct and transparent account of both the effectiveness and the current limitations of the replay mechanism.
